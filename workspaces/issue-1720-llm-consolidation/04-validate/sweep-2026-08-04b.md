## 5. Decision points

### A — #1996 premise is FALSE. Needs re-scope or a closure gate.

The issue says "unguarded module-level FastMCP import". **It is guarded** —
`transports/mcp.py:79` wraps it in `try/except ImportError`, logs at INFO, and
returns. Verified this session.

- **Recommend: re-scope, do not close.** The real defect underneath is the one
  Decision B names — the MCP tier is UNDECLARED, so behaviour depends on what
  happens to be installed. The issue's _value_ is real; its _premise_ is wrong.
- **Con of re-scoping:** rewriting an issue's premise loses the original
  reporter's framing. Mitigated by editing rather than closing.
- **Con of closing:** the underlying undeclared-tier problem loses its tracker.
- Closure would need the `value-prioritization.md` MUST-4 user gate regardless.

### B — release sequencing, now that convergence is NOT available

The ratified ordering was W19 → **Round-2 convergence** → versions → `/release`.
Convergence is unreachable: the loop hit the circuit breaker with the counter at
zero.

- **Recommend: release on ABNORMAL-TERMINATION evidence, not convergence** —
  and say so in the release notes. Basis: severity fell monotonically across
  seven rounds; nothing in the last three changed what the module leaks; both
  lanes independently endorsed stopping; every BUG-class finding is fixed and
  regression-pinned.
- **Pros:** 89 commits of real fixes reach users; the residuals are documented,
  pinned and self-clearing.

  because findings got small" is a weaker claim than "two clean rounds". A

  runaway the cap exists to prevent, and round 7 found debt in _adjacent_ code.
