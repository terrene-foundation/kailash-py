### Decision A — the 70 spec-drift findings (52 orphans + 18 coverage gaps)

Concentrated in the ML spec family: `ml-feature-store` (8), `ml-tracking` (7),
`ml-engines-v2` (6), `ml-registry` (5), then a long tail. Sample evidence:
`dataflow.lineage` — _"ast.walk found no ClassDef/FunctionDef/Assign named 'lineage'"_;
`EngineInfo.version` — _"no candidate source files exist for module path"_.

These were **invisible until this sweep** because the instrument was scanning nothing.

- **Option A1 — triage all 70 now.** Pro: closes the class; spec/code truth restored.
  Con: 2–3 cycles, unrelated to this workspace's scope, and an unknown share are likely
  false positives (attribute-style refs like `dataflow.hash` look lexical).
- **Option A2 — sample-then-scope (recommended).** Adjudicate ~10 rows across the top 3
  specs to establish the true-positive rate, then size the real work. Pro: 0.5 cycle to a
  defensible estimate; avoids committing 3 cycles to a set that may be mostly noise.
  Con: leaves 70 findings open one more cycle; if the rate is high, the real work is merely
  deferred, not avoided.
- **Option A3 — defer wholesale to the ML workstream.** Pro: zero cost now.
  Con: they are already-latent drift that just became visible — deferring the moment they
  surface is the decay pattern `value-prioritization.md` blocks.

**Recommendation: A2.** The honest blocker is that nobody knows the true-positive rate,
and every sizing of A1 vs A3 is a guess until ~10 rows are adjudicated. A2 buys that number
cheaply. **Confidence: MEDIUM** — the sample size is a judgment call; a 10-row sample on a
70-row set is indicative, not tight.
